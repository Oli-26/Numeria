## Contradicted claims to review (5)

These were flagged by a source during finding and have not been edited. Check each by hand before changing a lesson.

- `hist-afr-01-c1`: "fossils about 300,000 years old"  
  Source says: Thermoluminescence dating gives 315 ± 34 thousand years; 'about 300,000' is a loose rounding, not a contradiction. (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28593953/)
- `hist-exp-01-c2`: "The Marquesas, Society Islands, and Hawai'i by ~700–1000 CE."  
  Source says: Wilmshurst et al. 2011: East Polynesian colonization was earliest in the Society Islands at A.D. ~1025-1120, four centuries later than previously assumed, then one major pulse to all remaining islands A.D. ~1190-1290. So the 700-1000 CE dates are too early. (https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/high-precision-radiocarbon-dating-shows-recent-and-rapid-initial-/)
- `hist-em-01-c1`: "When Constantinople fell in 1453, Greek scholars carried manuscripts west."  
  Source says: Royal Holloway (J. Harris, 2019 talk): it is a perennial myth that scholars escaped with Greek classics under their arms. Many Italians learned Greek without Byzantine help, though exiles did affect Greek studies in Italy. The lesson overstates a sudden 1453 flight of scholars. (https://pure.royalholloway.ac.uk/en/activities/byzantium-and-the-renaissance)
- `hist-ind-01-c2`: "By 1800 there were roughly 500 Boulton & Watt engines operating across Britain"  
  Source says: By 1800, when Watt and Boultons partnership ended, 451 engines had been built of which 268 were rotative. (451 built, not roughly 500, and not all necessarily operating in Britain; some went overseas.) (https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/objects/co50950/model-boulton-and-watt-condensing-engine-c-1800)
- `hist-em-01-c3`: "Without Gutenberg, it wouldn't have been more than another obscure academic dispute. / The printing press directly enabled the Protestant Reformation."  
  Source says: Not contradicted directly; the source supports rapid printing of the theses (within two weeks, without Luther's permission). Search also noted Gutenberg's technical advances are usually dated to the 1450s for mass printing, so the c. 1440 date may need softening. (https://christianhistoryinstitute.org/magazine/article/preaching-from-the-print-shop)
