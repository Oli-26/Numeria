## Contradicted by a source (4)

These are likely errors in the lessons.

- `med-bias-01-c2`: "Tim Groseclose and Jeffrey Milyo's 2005 paper in the Quarterly Journal of Economics measured congressional citations by think tanks of various ideological scores"  
  Source says: Direction is reversed. The authors count how often media outlets cite think tanks, and compare with how often members of Congress cite the same think tanks in floor speeches, to build an ADA score per outlet. (Source is the 2003 working paper version; published as QJE 120(4), 2005, doi 10.1162/003355305775097542, whose page could not be read.) (https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~lpearl/courses/readings/GrosecloseMilyo2003_MediaBias.pdf)
- `med-bias-01-c2`: "The resulting "ADA score" placed most major US outlets to the left of the median voter, with Fox News Special Report as a clear conservative outlier."  
  Source says: Working-paper abstract: "All of the news outlets except Fox News' Special Report received a score to the left of the average member of Congress." The benchmark is the average member of Congress, not the median voter, and all but one outlet (not merely most) were left of it. Also, by one measure the Drudge Report was most centrist, and by the other Special Report was. (https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~lpearl/courses/readings/GrosecloseMilyo2003_MediaBias.pdf)
- `med-dig-01-c2`: "The San Jose Mercury News launched the first major online newspaper edition in 1994"  
  Source says: Columbia Journalism Review: Mercury Center launched in 1993 as an online service (when only about a dozen newspapers had online versions), and in January 1995 became 'the first news site in the nation to migrate to the web'. No 1994 launch. Search results also show the Palo Alto Weekly claiming first newspaper on the web in January 1994. (https://www.cjr.org/?p=97991)
- `med-dig-01-c1`: "Usenet launched in 1980 at Duke and the University of North Carolina"  
  Source says: Duke Today: Usenet newsgroups were 'started in 1979 by two Duke graduate students, Tom Truscott and Jim Ellis', working with a UNC-Chapel Hill graduate student. Public/official start is often given as 1980, so the lesson could say 'conceived in 1979, launched in 1980'. (https://today.duke.edu/2010/05/usenet.html)
