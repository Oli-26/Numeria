  Source says: The line reads 'And murmuring of innumerable bees.' with no 'The'; the lesson's quotation should be corrected to 'murmuring of innumerable bees'. (https://rpo.library.utoronto.ca/poems/princess-come-down-o-maid)

(https://www.iwm.org.uk/history/5-things-you-need-to-know-about-the-first-world-war)

## Rejected (5)

- `lit-myth-01-c3` "A legend is set in historical or quasi-historical time and treated as having hap": reviewer: Page exists and quotes Bascom (true to narrator, recent past, human heroes), but it is a secondary faculty FAQ with no named author on the page (Michael Webster is unverified). The claim's 'remarkable events' and 'quasi-historical time' go beyond what the page says. Better cited to Bascom 1965 directly. (https://faculty.gvsu.edu/WEBSTERM/MythFAQs.htm)
- `lit-poetry-01-c2` "When Tennyson writes 'The murmuring of innumerable bees,' the soft m's and r's e": reviewer: Tennyson's line on the page is 'And murmuring of innumerable bees.', but the lesson quotes 'The murmuring of innumerable bees,' so the quote is mismatched. The page is only the poem text and says nothing about m and r sounds enacting the sense. (https://rpo.library.utoronto.ca/poems/princess-come-down-o-maid)
- `lit-poetry-01-c2` "When Hopkins writes 'shook foil,' the words flash like the metal they name": reviewer: The page confirms the words 'shook foil' in the poem. It does not say they flash like the metal, so the sound-sense claim is interpretation that the source doesn't support. (https://poets.org/poem/gods-grandeur)
- `lit-pc-01-c2` "In Culture and Imperialism (1993), Edward Said showed how the novel registers an": reviewer: Page exists (Nina Mehta, CSM, 12 Apr 1993) and covers Said, Mansfield Park and the Antigua plantation. But the quoted fragment only says the plantation underwrote the Bertrams' security and authority. The claim that the novel 'registers and obscures' the slavery was not confirmed on the page, and a newspaper report is a weak source for that interpretive claim. (https://www.csmonitor.com/1993/0412/12132.html)
- `lit-pc-01-c3` "His major books were Black Skin, White Masks (1952), on the psychological effect": reviewer: SEP confirms the dates (Black Skin, White Masks 1952, The Wretched of the Earth 1961). It frames Black Skin around anti-Black racism, not 'the psychological effects of colonisation', so the claim is broader than the source. The quote given covers only the 1952 date, not the whole claim. (https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/frantz-fanon/)
