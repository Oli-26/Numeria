## Contradicted by a source (6)

These are likely errors in the lessons.

- `geo-volc-01-c3`: "Most of Earth's ocean floor is covered in pillow lava."  
  Source says: Moore (USGS) says 'Much of the ocean floor' is covered by pillow lava and that the 'great bulk of new ocean floor created at diverging plate boundaries' is pillowed basalt, but it is mantled by sediment. 'Most of the ocean floor' is a stronger claim than 'much'; suggest softening to 'Much of'. (https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70207937)
- `geo-volc-01-c2`: "Basaltic magma (~50 % SiO₂): low viscosity, high temperature (~1200 °C)"  
  Source says: Tulane gives ranges: basaltic SiO2 45-55 wt% at 1000-1200 C; rhyolitic SiO2 65-75% at 650-800 C. Lesson values are the upper ends of the ranges; consider saying 'up to ~1200 C' and 'up to ~800 C'. (https://www2.tulane.edu/~sanelson/Natural_Disasters/volcan&magma.htm)
- `geo-tec-01-c3`: "It's now thought to be the dominant force, so the plates themselves help drive the convection."  
  Source says: Conrad and Lithgow-Bertelloni (2002, Science, 'How mantle slabs drive plate tectonics') say slab pull forces 'account for about half of the total driving force on plates', and that lower-mantle slabs drive plates through slab suction. So slab pull is the largest single force, but not clearly dominant, and the 'so the plates help drive convection' inference is not stated. Suggest softening to 'one of the largest forces'. (http://kgblab.epss.ucla.edu/publication/conrad-s-298-207-2002/)
- `astb-01-c2`: "In 1992 a NASA Exobiology working group, anchored by Gerald Joyce, settled on a practical phrase that researchers still cite"  
  Source says: The SEP Life entry attributes the NASA definition to Joyce 1994, a foreword in Deamer & Fleischaker (eds.), Origins of Life: the Central Concepts. It does not mention a working group or the year 1992. The lesson's date should be checked, and 1994 is the date the opened source gives. (https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/life/)
- `geo-met-01-c1`: "Iron comes overwhelmingly from hematite (Fe₂O₃) and magnetite (Fe₃O₄), at about 2.5 billion tonnes of ore a year."  
  Source says: USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 (Iron Ore) lists world total mine production (usable ore, rounded) as 2,600,000 thousand metric tons, i.e. about 2.6 billion tonnes, for both 2024 and 2025e. The lesson's 2.5 billion is slightly low; suggest 2.6 billion. (https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-iron-ore.pdf)
- `geo-met-01-c3`: "About 70 % comes from the Democratic Republic of the Congo as a copper by-product."  
  Source says: USGS says Congo (Kinshasa) accounted for an estimated 73% of world mined cobalt (2025), so 'about 70%' is a little low; suggest 'about 73%'. The by-product-of-copper wording was not stated in the excerpt I read, only that resources lie in sediment-hosted stratiform copper deposits in Congo and Zambia. (https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-cobalt.pdf)
