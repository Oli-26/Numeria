- `chem-pharm-01-c1`: "The druggable genome, the proteins that small molecules can modulate, is estimated at ~3000 of the ~20,000 human proteins."  
  Source says: Finan et al. 2017 (Sci Transl Med 9:eaag1166, doi 10.1126/scitranslmed.aag1166; PDF copy opened, text extracted): 'We estimated 4479 (22%) of the 20,300 protein-coding genes annotated in Ensembl version 73 to be drugged or druggable. This adds 2282 genes to previous estimates made by Hopkins and Groom, Russ and Lampel, or Kumar et al.' So ~3000 is an older estimate (about 2200 in earlier work); current estimate is ~4500 (22%), and it includes biologic-accessible targets. (https://shicheng-guo.github.io/research/The%20druggable%20genome%20and%20support%20for%20target%20identification%20and%20validation%20in%20drug%20development..pdf)
