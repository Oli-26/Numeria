## Contradicted by a source (1)

These are likely errors in the lessons.

- `phil-01-c2`: "Hippasus discovered that the diagonal of a unit square can't be expressed as a ratio of whole numbers; he was drowned for revealing this secret."  
  Source says: SEP (section 3.4) says 'neither does any ancient source connect Hippasus to the discovery of the irrational'. The drowning report (Iamblichus, VP 88) concerns making public a dodecahedron construction, not irrationality. The lesson's 'legend' hedge helps, but the link to the square diagonal and to irrationality is not ancient-source supported. (https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pythagoreanism/)

## Rejected (8)

- `phil-meta-01-c3` "Everything else is contingent: it has an essence but needn't have existed.": reviewer: The quote only says God's simplicity excludes essence/existence composition in God. The page does not use 'contingent' or say everything else needn't have existed, so the claim is broader than the source. (https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/aquinas/)
- `phil-mind-01-c1` "He proposed that they interact via the pineal gland, a hypothesis now considered": reviewer: Quote only says Descartes' anatomical assumptions were mistaken; it does not state the pineal interaction hypothesis is considered false. The page also doubts Descartes was a plain interactionist, so the claim is not matched exactly. (https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2015/entries/pineal-gland/)
- `phil-mind-01-c2` "Berkeley's slogan is 'esse est percipi', to be is to be perceived.": reviewer: The quote is on the page, but the author is wrong. The SEP Berkeley entry is not by Tom Stoneham, and the page showed no byline. The attribution is unverified.; quote not found on page (https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/berkeley/)
- `phil-01-c1` "The number 10, the tetraktys (1+2+3+4), was sacred enough to swear oaths upon.": reviewer: The quoted passage says Pythagoreans swore oaths by Pythagoras, who handed down the tetraktys. It does not say the tetraktys was sacred or was sworn by. Only the page's description of 1+2+3+4 and the perfect number ten matches, so the oath claim is unsupported. (https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pythagoras/)
- `phil-01-c2` "Legend has it that Hippasus was drowned at sea for revealing this secret, though": reviewer: The SEP Pythagoreanism page says Hippasus drowned for making public a diagram of the dodecahedron, not for revealing the irrationality of the square root of 2. The lesson claim concerns the irrationality secret, so the source does not support it exactly. (https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pythagoreanism/)
- `phil-ph-01-c1` "Edmund Husserl (1859–1938) launched phenomenology with a slogan, 'Zu den Sachen ": reviewer: SEP Husserl page has the 'things themselves' quote (Logical Investigations) but does not mention the slogan 'Zu den Sachen selbst' or present it as the slogan launching phenomenology. (https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/husserl/)
- `phil-ph-01-c2` "The noesis is the act of consciousness, such as perceiving, remembering or doubt": quote not found on page (https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/phenomenology/)
- `pomo-01-c1` "only crystallized with Jean-François Lyotard's La condition postmoderne (1979), ": reviewer: The SEP page confirms the Quebec commission. It does not support the broader claim that the philosophical sense 'only crystallized' with this 1979 work. The claim also uses the French name Conseil des universités du Québec, which the quote does not. (https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/lyotard/)
