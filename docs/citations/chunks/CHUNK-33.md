## Rejected (7)

- `misc-con-01-c3` "A moon-landing hoax (~411,000 people) would have leaked within 4 years": reviewer: Paper and quote are real, but the page text I could fetch gives no 411,000 figure or ~4-year estimate for the moon hoax (those are in image tables). The quote only says 'hundreds of thousands ... less than half a decade', which is broader than the claim. (https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0147905)
- `misc-con-01-c3` "Even with extreme operational discipline, conspiracies of more than a few thousa": reviewer: The quote is real, but it says conspiracies of 1000 or more agents quickly become untenable. The claim says 'more than a few thousand' and 'leak within years' under 'extreme operational discipline', which differs in threshold and adds a qualifier the quote doesn't support. (https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0147905)
- `cult-01-c1` "Stark and Bainbridge in The Future of Religion (1985) used 'cult' purely structu": reviewer: The page exists and quotes Stark and Bainbridge (1985: 25-26), but the quote is about a cult being novel relative to the society's other religious bodies. The page does not say 'purely structurally' or 'lacking organisational continuity'. It also gives no support for the claim that Stark and Bainbridge used the term structurally. The source is a secondary, preliminary conference paper that reads the definition as novelty, not organisational continuity. The claim is narrower and different in emphasis than what the page supports. (https://cesnur.org/2003/vil2003_doktor.htm)
- `hypr-01-c3` "A bank panic is a self-fulfilling prophecy.": DOI not found in Crossref (https://sociologica.unibo.it/article/download/13653/15729?inline=1)
- `misc-lc-01-c1` "No earlier Greek source mentions it, and neither does any Egyptian, Mesopotamian": reviewer: Page says no place like Atlantis appears in the pre-Classical tradition, but does not say no Egyptian, Mesopotamian or other contemporary text mentions it. The claim is broader than the source, and the quote is not verbatim. (https://www.penn.museum/sites/expedition/atlantis-lost-and-found/)
- `misc-lc-01-c1` "Most classical scholars consider Atlantis a moral parable that Plato invented, a": reviewer: Page gives this as the author's own view and does not say most classical scholars hold it. The quote supports only 'Atlantis never existed', not the consensus claim or the 'mirror for Athenian vices' part. (https://www.penn.museum/sites/expedition/atlantis-lost-and-found/)
- `misc-mem-01-c2` "Variation means the copies differ, heredity means offspring resemble parents, an": reviewer: The SEP entry exists, but the quote covers only variation and fitness differences. The claim defines three terms, including heredity as 'offspring resemble parents', which the quote does not support. The page's own wording on heredity differs. The year 2018 does not match the entry (first published 2001, revised 2022). (https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/replication/)
