## Contradicted by a source (2)

These are likely errors in the lessons.

- `hist-em-01-c1`: "When Constantinople fell in 1453, Greek scholars carried manuscripts west."  
  Source says: Jonathan Harris (Royal Holloway, 2019 talk) calls the story that scholars fled Constantinople carrying Greek classics and kickstarted Greek studies in Italy a 'perennial myth' that modern scholarship has largely undermined. Greek teaching in Florence predates 1453 (Chrysoloras, 1397). Lesson should soften this. (https://pure.royalholloway.ac.uk/en/activities/byzantium-and-the-renaissance)
- `hist-em-01-c3`: "By 1500, over 20 million books circulated in Europe."  
  Source says: Vassar gives about 20 million copies by 1501, but this is an estimate; searches show scholarly estimates range from about 8 to 20+ million (CERL), so lesson should say 'an estimated 20 million' or 'roughly 10-20 million'. Britannica reportedly gives over 9 million; not opened. (https://www.vassar.edu/specialcollections/exhibit-highlights/2001-2005/Incunabula/patkus.html)

## Rejected (4)

- `hist-em-01-c3` "By 1500, over 20 million books circulated in Europe.": reviewer: Page says approximately 30,000 editions and around 20 million copies for 1455-1501, not 'over 20 million by 1500'. Claim is slightly stronger than the source, and the page has no stated year. (https://www.vassar.edu/specialcollections/exhibit-highlights/2001-2005/Incunabula/patkus.html)
- `hist-em-01-c2` "Michelangelo (1475–1564) painted the Sistine Chapel ceiling (1508–1512)": reviewer: Page supports completion by 31 Oct 1512 only. It gives no 1508 start date and no Michelangelo life dates (1475-1564), so the claim is broader than the quote. (https://www.museivaticani.va/content/museivaticani/en/collezioni/musei/cappella-sistina/volta.html)
- `hist-econ-01-c3` "Coined money emerged in Lydia (around 600 BCE)": reviewer: The page gives no date of c. 600 BCE for coinage. It says Lydia was 'one of the first states to mint coinage, perhaps in the reign of Alyattes', and the timeline range is c. 635-585 BCE, so the claim is only loosely supported. World History Encyclopedia is also a general encyclopedia site, a weak source for this claim. The quote is a timeline entry, not a statement that coined money emerged in Lydia around 600 BCE. (https://www.worldhistory.org/Lydia/)
