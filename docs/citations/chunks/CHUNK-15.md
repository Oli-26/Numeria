## Rejected (6)

- `hist-afr-01-c1` "At Jebel Irhoud in Morocco, fossils about 300,000 years old are anatomically mod": reviewer: Page and quote verified, but the abstract says Jebel Irhoud documents early stages of the H. sapiens lineage with key modern features. The claim 'anatomically modern Homo sapiens' is stronger than that, and the quoted 315 ± 34 ka age does not support it. (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28593953/)
- `hist-afr-01-c2` "Carthage was founded by Phoenicians around 800 BCE and became a major Mediterran": reviewer: The quote covers only the founding date (c. 814 BCE), not the major-power or 146 BCE destruction parts of the claim. World History Encyclopedia is also a general tertiary encyclopedia, not a clearly authoritative source. (https://www.worldhistory.org/carthage/)
- `hist-afr-01-c3` "Mali's ruler Mansa Musa made a famous hajj in 1324 and displayed wealth on a sca": reviewer: The quote about a 20% gold dinar drop in Cairo is on the page, but the page gives a 12-year recovery, so 'for years' is only loosely supported. The source is a general tertiary encyclopedia, and the 'hajj in 1324' wording is not on the page. Too doubtful to accept. (https://www.worldhistory.org/Mansa_Musa_I/)
- `hist-anc-01-c1` "Cuneiform writing (wedge-marks pressed into clay) around 3200 BCE, one of the ea": reviewer: Page is real and the quote is verbatim, but the metadata is wrong: it is by Ruth E. Kott, 2011, not 2002. The page dates protocuneiform to about 3200 BC, which is slightly narrower than the claim's cuneiform date. (http://magazine.uchicago.edu/1102/features/the_origins_of_writing.shtml)
- `hist-anc-01-c1` "The oldest surviving law code, Ur-Nammu's (c. 2100 BCE)": reviewer: The page is a marketing blog for Tulane's online law program, not an authoritative source. The title differs, it gives no c. 2100 BCE date, and it says 'oldest written law' where the claim says 'oldest surviving law code'. (https://online.law.tulane.edu/blog/law-in-the-ancient-world)
- `hdec-01-c2` "the Phoenician and Luwian hieroglyphic texts at Karatepe in Turkey, found in the": reviewer: Page supports the bilingual's role in decipherment, but gives only the site's discovery in 1946 and excavations 1947-51. It does not date the bilingual's finding to the late 1940s, so the date is not exactly supported. (https://hittitemonuments.com/karatepe/)
