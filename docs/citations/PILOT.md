## Contradicted by a source (2)

These are likely errors in the lessons.

- `gnoc-01-c3`: "In Krstić (2004), the ICTY ruled that the 8,000 men and boys of Srebrenica constituted a "substantial part" of Bosnian Muslims, sustaining a genocide conviction."  
  Source says: The Appeals Chamber treated the Bosnian Muslims of Srebrenica as the substantial part of the group, and the killing of the men was evidence of intent to destroy that wider community. The defence argued the targeted part was the military-age men; the Chamber noted the massacred men were about one fifth of the Srebrenica community. Also, Krstić's conviction was changed on appeal to aiding and abetting genocide. (https://www.icty.org/x/cases/krstic/acjug/en/krs-aj040419e.htm)
- `bio-gen-01-c2`: "Your genome contains roughly 20,000–25,000 protein-coding genes."  
  Source says: NHGRI: "Humans have approximately 20,000 protein-coding genes." A 2014 Human Molecular Genetics paper (doi 10.1093/hmg/ddu309, Ezkurdia et al.) says the human genome likely has fewer than 20,000, close to 19,000. The 25,000 upper bound is outdated; suggest "about 20,000". (https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Gene)
