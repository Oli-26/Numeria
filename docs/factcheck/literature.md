## Systemic notes

- The dominant error type is invented or misattributed quotations and critical claims, not dates: an invented Henry James line on George Eliot, an invented Nabokov line on Gogol, a spliced non-Joycean "Ulysses" passage, Horace's "Graecia capta" credited to Cicero, a nonsensical "Polish-British conversation" behind The Waste Land, Henry James "emulating" Shakespeare's late style. Any new quote in the literature field deserves a source check.
- Second pattern: plausible-sounding plot details that are wrong (Casaubon's "withered hand", a Dubliners story about a sister's funeral, Mrs Dalloway "ending with the protagonist's death", O'Neill's "tubercular brother", Achilles dragging Hector "around the city", Onegin "travelling abroad").
- Nobel/Booker years are clean across all 19 topics (all ~45 prize mentions verified). Dates are mostly right; the few date slips (534 vs 525 BCE for tragedy, War and Peace 1867, Woolf's "1923" essay, Seagull revival "the next year") were internal inconsistencies as much as errors.
- Two items were outdated: Ngugi (died 2025) still listed as living in lit-postcolonial, and Krasznahorkai (Nobel 2025) presented as an under-read writer in lit-comparative.
- lit-romanticism, lit-short-fiction and lit-world (questions rewritten yesterday) were noticeably cleaner: one fix each. The older, denser topics (lit-classical-antiquity, lit-modernism, lit-novel, lit-comparative) carried most errors.
- Several MC items across topics use joke distractors ("It is invisible", "Fiction about cars"); not factual errors, left alone.
- All edited sentences were rewritten without em dashes; pre-existing em dashes elsewhere were not touched.
