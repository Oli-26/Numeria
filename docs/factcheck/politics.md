## Systemic notes

- Politics topics were generally accurate on theory and canonical history; errors were concentrated in dense 'survey' paragraphs listing many dates and names (security, empire, revolutions), e.g. Lincoln listed as an anarchist assassination, Milošević as an ICC indictee, 'British' recognition of Algerian independence, '2 million American/Korean' Vietnam deaths, an Israeli role in India's bomb.
- Currency drift since 2024-25: Orbán's April 2026 defeat, Timor-Leste joining ASEAN (11 members), Bulgaria joining the euro (21 members), New START expiry, India suspending the Indus Waters Treaty, GEC closure. Hungary is still used throughout as the model backsliding case, which remains valid historically; only present-tense claims were updated.
- Contested political questions are mostly framed as contested (US backsliding debate, identity politics, populism causes). Two places asserted a contested judgement as settled and were rephrased neutrally: the Williams slavery-and-industrialisation thesis ('broadly supported') and the Israel-Palestine 'no single party's bad faith' line.
- No FindTheError items exist in these fields; MC distractors were generally unambiguous. One FillIn (pol-th-q12) tested a word ('yet') absent from the Burke quotation it cited.
- politics-security and politics-empire are written in a generic, repetitive register ('one of the central questions of the period') that hides thin specifics; worth a later content pass.
