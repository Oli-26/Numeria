## Systemic notes

- Recurring popular myths, several repeated across topics: the Ottomans "blocking" Asian trade and so causing the Age of Discovery (early-modern, exploration; medieval still carries the framing); Magellan "proving" the Earth round; Tordesillas "brokered by the Pope"; cuius regio attributed to Westphalia instead of Augsburg (early-modern, religion); apocryphal quotes presented as genuine ("L'Etat, c'est moi", Bismarck's "killing socialism with kindness", "first, do no harm" in the Hippocratic Oath); ARPANET built to survive nuclear war; Copernicus "simpler" than Ptolemy; floating chinampas; Sagres navigation school; Harrison "awarded the prize".
- Casualty figures were inconsistent across topics and sometimes picked one side of a contested range: Partition deaths (twentieth said up to 1M, south-asian 1-2M, a question 200k-2M), Holodomor (russian 4-7M vs genocide-atrocity 3.5-5M), Congo Free State ("conservative estimate 10M"), Algerian War (1.5M, the Algerian state figure), Congo wars (5M). These were aligned to ranges and labelled as contested.
- FindTheError items in the newer, argument-heavy topics (environmental, warfare, genocide-atrocity, historiography) often had a second false step before or beside the keyed one. 12 items were repaired by rewriting the extra false steps as true premises; the keyed answer was changed only for histr-q64, to match the "first erroneous step" convention the other items use.
- Time-sensitive claims go stale fast in history-modern, history-environmental and history-exploration (planetary boundaries, seafloor mapping, AIDS deaths, "fastest-growing app", Hagia Sophia status, AH year). Worth a scheduled refresh.
- Topic quality: the core survey topics (ancient, twentieth, russian, genocide-atrocity) are strong; early-modern, exploration and industrial carried the most textbook myths; environmental and warfare carried most of the FindTheError defects; modern had the most dated or mis-sequenced recent facts.
- Several figcaptions do not match their concept (e.g. Bayeux Tapestry in a Byzantine concept, Alhambra under the Mughals, Aldrin on the Moon under Mao). They are not false, so they were left alone.
